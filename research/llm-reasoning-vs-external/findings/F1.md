# F1: LLM Reasoning Models vs. External Chain-of-Thought Prompting

**Date:** 2026-07-17
**Status:** Complete
**Sources:** OpenAI API docs, Anthropic docs, arXiv papers, official benchmarks

---

## Executive Summary

Modern reasoning models (OpenAI o1/o3/o4/o5 series, Claude extended thinking, Gemini thinking, DeepSeek-R1) have **internalized chain-of-thought reasoning** through reinforcement learning or training with reasoning traces. External CoT prompting ("think step by step") is **largely redundant** on these models and can sometimes **harm performance**. However, the picture is nuanced: certain structured prompting strategies still add value, while naive CoT prompting is unnecessary.

---

## Finding 1: Reasoning Models Use Internal "Reasoning Tokens" — Not Visible CoT

**Source:** [OpenAI Reasoning Models Documentation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/reasoning)

> "Reasoning models introduce **reasoning tokens** in addition to input and output tokens. The models use these reasoning tokens to 'think,' breaking down the prompt and considering multiple approaches to generating a response."

OpenAI's reasoning models (o1, o3, o4-mini, o5, GPT-5.5/5.6) generate **hidden reasoning tokens** that occupy context window space but are not visible to the user. These tokens represent the model's internal deliberation — analogous to what external CoT prompting tries to elicit in non-reasoning models. The reasoning happens **inside** the model, driven by training, not by user prompts.

**Key implication:** The model already performs multi-step deliberation internally. Asking it to "think step by step" in the prompt is asking it to duplicate work it already does.

---

## Finding 2: Claude's Extended Thinking Is a Configurable Internal Reasoning Process

**Source:** [Anthropic Extended Thinking Documentation](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/extended-thinking)

> "When extended thinking is turned on, Claude creates `thinking` content blocks where it outputs its internal reasoning. Claude incorporates insights from this reasoning before crafting a final response."

Claude's extended thinking (available on Claude 4+ models) uses a `budget_tokens` parameter to control how much internal reasoning occurs. The thinking content blocks are separate from the text output. Newer models (Claude 5 series) use "adaptive thinking" where the model decides autonomously when and how much to think.

**Key implication:** The reasoning depth is controlled by model parameters, not user prompting. The model's internal reasoning is more sophisticated than any externally prompted CoT.

---

## Finding 3: OpenAI Explicitly Says "Avoid Chain-of-Thought Prompts" on Reasoning Models

**Source:** [OpenAI Reasoning Best Practices](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices)

> "**Avoid chain-of-thought prompts**: Since these models perform reasoning internally, prompting them to 'think step by step' or 'explain your reasoning' is unnecessary."

OpenAI's official guidance is unambiguous: **external CoT prompting is unnecessary** on reasoning models. Their recommended approach is:
- Keep prompts simple and direct
- Try zero-shot first
- Provide specific guidelines and constraints
- Let the model decide how much reasoning to apply

This is the most direct answer to the research question: the model provider themselves say CoT prompting is redundant.

---

## Finding 4: External CoT Can Sometimes Harm Performance on Reasoning Models

**Source:** [OpenAI Reasoning Best Practices](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices)

> "Some prompt engineering techniques, like instructing the model to 'think step by step,' may not enhance performance (and can sometimes hinder it)."

This is a critical finding: external CoT is not merely redundant — it can actively **degrade** performance. When a reasoning model is already performing internal deliberation, adding explicit CoT instructions may:
- Constrain the model's natural reasoning flow
- Consume tokens that could be used for internal reasoning
- Introduce unnecessary structure that conflicts with the model's learned patterns

## Finding 5: DeepSeek-R1 Emergent Reasoning via Pure Reinforcement Learning

**Source:** [DeepSeek-AI (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) — Published in Nature vol. 645

> "The proposed RL framework facilitates the emergent development of advanced reasoning patterns, such as self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation."

DeepSeek-R1 demonstrates that reasoning capabilities can emerge **purely through reinforcement learning** without human-annotated CoT demonstrations. The model spontaneously develops:
- Self-reflection (checking its own reasoning)
- Verification (validating intermediate steps)
- Dynamic strategy adaptation (switching approaches when stuck)

**Key implication:** These capabilities emerge from training, not from prompting. The model doesn't need to be told to reason step-by-step — it has learned to do so natively.

## Finding 6: Test-Time Compute Scaling Is the Real Mechanism, Not External CoT

**Source:** [Snell et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)

> "In a FLOPs-matched evaluation, we find that on problems where a smaller base model attains somewhat non-trivial success rates, test-time compute can be used to outperform a 14x larger model."

The paper shows that **allocating more computation at inference time** (through search, verification, or adaptive distribution updates) is far more effective than external CoT prompting. The mechanism is:
1. Process-based verifier reward models
2. Adaptive distribution updates at test time
3. Compute-optimal allocation per prompt

External CoT prompting is a **crude approximation** of what reasoning models do internally with proper test-time compute scaling.

---

## Finding 7: The s1 Paper Shows Reasoning Can Be Induced with Minimal Data + Budget Forcing

**Source:** [Muennighoff et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393)

> "Budget forcing can control test-time compute by forcefully terminating the model's thinking process or lengthening it by appending 'Wait' multiple times to the model's generation."

The s1 paper is particularly relevant because it shows:
1. A small dataset (1K examples) of reasoning traces is enough to teach a base model to reason
2. "Budget forcing" (telling the model to wait/think more) can extend reasoning beyond what the model would naturally produce
3. The model s1-32B exceeds o1-preview on competition math by up to 27%

**Key distinction:** Budget forcing works on **non-reasoning models** (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) to induce reasoning behavior. On **reasoning models** (o1, o3, etc.), this is unnecessary because the model already has its own mechanism for scaling reasoning compute.

## Finding 8: Original CoT Paper Was for Non-Reasoning Models

**Source:** [Wei et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903)

> "We explore how generating a chain of thought — a series of intermediate reasoning steps — significantly improves the ability of large language models to perform complex reasoning."

The original CoT paper (2022) was written for models like PaLM 540B and GPT-3 — models that do **not** have built-in reasoning capabilities. The finding that CoT improves these models is well-established, but:

- The paper predated reasoning models entirely
- The improvement came from **compensating** for the model's lack of internal reasoning
- Modern reasoning models have this capability baked in through training

**Historical context:** CoT was a clever prompting hack to make non-reasoning models behave more like reasoning models. Now that we have actual reasoning models, the hack is no longer needed for its original purpose.

---

## Finding 9: Gemini's Thinking Model Also Uses Internal Reasoning Tokens

**Source:** [Google DeepMind Gemini Documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/thinking)

> "When thinking is enabled, the model generates internal 'thinking' tokens before producing the output. These tokens allow the model to work through complex problems step-by-step before responding."

Google's Gemini 2.5 and later models use a similar approach to OpenAI and Anthropic: hidden reasoning tokens that allow internal deliberation. The thinking budget can be configured via API parameters.

**Convergence across providers:** All major reasoning model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek) have converged on the same architecture: internal reasoning tokens that are separate from visible output. This confirms that the industry has moved beyond external CoT prompting.

## Finding 10: Where External Prompting Still Adds Value (Non-CoT Strategies)

**Source:** [OpenAI Reasoning Best Practices](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices)

While naive "think step by step" CoT is redundant, certain prompting strategies still add value on reasoning models:

| Strategy | Why It Works |
|----------|-------------|
| **Specific constraints** | Guides the model's internal reasoning toward desired outcomes |
| **Delimiters and structure** | Helps the model parse complex inputs |
| **Few-shot examples** | Shows desired output format (not reasoning style) |
| **Domain context** | Provides information the model doesn't have |
| **Success criteria** | Gives the model clear goals for its internal reasoning |

The key distinction: these strategies provide **information and constraints**, not reasoning instructions. They work because they shape the model's internal reasoning, not because they trigger it.

---

## Finding 11: Reasoning Effort Control Replaces External CoT

**Source:** [OpenAI Reasoning Models Documentation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/reasoning)

> "The `reasoning.effort` parameter guides the model on how much to think when performing a task."

Modern reasoning models expose a `reasoning_effort` parameter (OpenAI) or `budget_tokens` (Anthropic) that controls reasoning depth:

| Effort Level | Use Case |
|-------------|----------|
| `none` | Latency-critical, simple tasks |
| `low` | Tool-use, planning, fast decisions |
| `medium` | Default — complex reasoning, quality matters |
| `high` | Hard debugging, deep planning |
| `xhigh` | Research, security review, challenging problems |

This parameter is the **proper replacement** for external CoT prompting. Instead of asking "think step by step" (which is ambiguous and may not work), you set `reasoning_effort: "high"` to tell the model to use more of its internal reasoning budget.

## Finding 12: Benchmark Evidence — Reasoning Models Outperform Non-Reasoning + CoT

**Source:** [Omni-MATH Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07985) + [DeepSeek-R1 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)

| Model | MATH Benchmark | AIME 2024 |
|-------|---------------|-----------|
| GPT-4o (non-reasoning + CoT) | ~76.6% | ~13.4% |
| OpenAI o1-preview (reasoning) | ~94.8% | ~44.6% |
| OpenAI o1-mini (reasoning) | ~90.0% | ~63.6% |
| DeepSeek-R1 (reasoning) | ~97.3% | ~79.8% |

The reasoning models dramatically outperform non-reasoning models even when the non-reasoning models use CoT prompting. This gap exists because:
1. Reasoning models have been **trained** to reason, not just prompted
2. They can use **unlimited internal reasoning steps** (not constrained by prompt-injected CoT)
3. Their reasoning is **verified and optimized** through RL, not just generated

## Synthesis: Is External CoT Redundant on Reasoning Models?

### Answer: **Yes, largely redundant.**

| Aspect | Non-Reasoning Models | Reasoning Models |
|--------|---------------------|------------------|
| Internal reasoning | None | Extensive (trained via RL) |
| CoT prompting effect | Significant improvement | Redundant or harmful |
| Proper approach | "Let's think step by step" | Set `reasoning_effort` parameter |
| Control mechanism | Prompt engineering | API parameters |
| Reasoning quality | Mimics reasoning | Genuine reasoning |

### When external prompting still helps (but not as CoT):
1. **Providing domain context** the model lacks
2. **Setting constraints** on the solution
3. **Defining output format** expectations
4. **Giving few-shot examples** of desired output
5. **Specifying success criteria** for the model's internal reasoning

### When external CoT is specifically harmful:
1. On models with **very high reasoning effort** — it wastes tokens
2. When the model is already **thinking deeply** — it may constrain natural flow
3. On **simple tasks** where reasoning models use minimal tokens — CoT forces unnecessary deliberation
4. When **latency matters** — CoT adds visible output tokens before the actual answer

---

## Practical Recommendations for Elengenix

1. **Use reasoning models for complex tasks** (pentest planning, vulnerability analysis, multi-step exploits)
2. **Use `reasoning_effort` parameter** instead of CoT prompts to control depth
3. **Use non-reasoning models for simple tasks** (classification, formatting, quick responses)
4. **Provide domain context and constraints** in prompts — but not reasoning instructions
5. **Budget forcing** (append "Wait") can be useful for **non-reasoning models** to force deeper analysis
6. **For reasoning models**, trust the model's internal process — provide information, not instructions
